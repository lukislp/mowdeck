# ADR 0058: Theft tracking mode

## Status

Accepted (2026-10-27)

## Context

[ADR 0027](0027-health-history-and-theft-alarm.md) raises critical theft alarms (`mower_lifted`, `mower_left_dock_unexpectedly`, `geofence_exit`) with one position, and the Device-Gateway reports a docked or idle Mammotion mower only every few minutes. A thief who carries the mower away needs a different rhythm: a position every few seconds over the 4G link, a way to show the police where it is without handing over the account, and a report to attach to the complaint. The owner decided: tracking starts by itself on a theft alarm and ends by hand ("Fehlalarm / Ortung beenden") or after 24 h; positions every 10 s (both configurable on the server); a secret share link that shows only a live map for 48 h; a PDF theft report.

## Decision

**Telemetry owns the sessions.** It already holds the position track, the mower directory and the mower routes, and it is the one service that reads positions; a new service for three tables would add a deployment, a database and CI jobs without a boundary worth drawing. Tables (migration 0012): `tracking_sessions` (a partial unique index allows one active session per mower), `tracking_links` (only the SHA-256 of the token), `tracking_photos`; `mower_directory` gains `name` and `serial` (the manufacturer's device name from the connection options, the closest to a serial number MowDeck knows). The session stores no positions: its track is the normal position track (`mower_positions`) between `started_at` and `ended_at`, which the gateway fills at the session's interval.

**State machine.** `active` until `ended_at`; end reasons `false_alarm`, `stopped` (by hand) and `max_duration` (`MOWDECK_TELEMETRY_TRACKING_MAX_HOURS`, 24). Starting is idempotent (a second alarm while tracking changes nothing). Start triggers: the three alarm kinds (Telemetry consumes `notifications.raised`, durable `telemetry-notifications`; `MOWDECK_TELEMETRY_TRACKING_AUTO_START` switches it off) or `POST /mowers/{id}/tracking` by hand. A background tick every `MOWDECK_TELEMETRY_TRACKING_TICK_SECONDS` (15) runs on one replica (PostgreSQL advisory lock) and, per active session: ends it at the maximum, follows the position, marks it **stale** when the mower has not reported for `..._STALE_AFTER_SECONDS` (120) or the link is down (the last known position stays; one notice per outage), notices a **move** of at least 50 m at most every 5 minutes, and renews the gateway lease.

**Gateway lease, not a command.** Telemetry asks the Device-Gateway that holds the mower, over core NATS `gateway.mower.<id>.tracking` (`TrackingLeaseRequest`/`Reply`), for reports every `interval_seconds` (10, never below 5) for `lease_seconds` (60), and renews it on every tick. When Telemetry dies the lease runs out and the mower returns to its normal rhythm, so a crash can never leave a mower polling the vendor cloud for ever. The session applies it through the new optional connection capability `TrackingRateCapable` (like `ReportRateCapable`, [ADR 0042](0042-drive-calibration.md)); the Mammotion adapter gets a `track` mode in its refresh loop: one undebounced report request per interval (cloud/4G or Bluetooth, whatever link is up), and the gateway heartbeat follows the interval so every report is a track point. A connection that reports on its own (the simulator) needs no port. Replies: `ok`, `not_found` (also for a foreign account), `not_connected` (the next renewal applies it when the mower is back).

**Vendor limits.** About 0.1 requests per second per tracked mower is far below the 1 per second of manual driving that the vendor server accepted ([ADR 0049](0049-manual-driving-over-the-mammotion-cloud.md)). The adapter additionally backs off: three consecutive polls without a new report double the wait (cap 120 s), a failed request counts as silent and is logged at warning level (the first and every tenth), a report resets it. The lease is the only way to start it, and it ends by itself.

**Notifications.** Telemetry publishes `tracking.started`, `.moved`, `.stale`, `.ended` (`event.tracking.*`, `TrackingEvent`); Notifications consumes them (durable `notifications-tracking`) and creates the kinds `tracking_started` (critical), `tracking_moved`, `tracking_stale` (warning) and `tracking_ended` (info). All but the end ignore quiet hours; they have no acknowledge silence (the end of the session is the answer) and no cooldown of their own: Telemetry sends no spam (one start, a move at most every 5 minutes, one stale notice per outage, one end). They open `/mowers/<id>/tracking`; ending a session from the notification itself needs a service worker action and is not part of this change (the page opens with the "false alarm" button one tap away).

**Rights** follow [ADR 0027](0027-health-history-and-theft-alarm.md) and need no model change: reading the session, the links and the report need `can_view`; starting, ending, taking photos, creating and revoking links and deleting need `can_control` (a viewer must not publish the location). Acknowledging an alarm needs only the inbox item.

**Share link.** The owner creates a link for the active or newest session: 256 bits from the CSPRNG, shown once, only its SHA-256 stored, expiry 48 h (`..._LINK_TTL_HOURS`; a caller may ask for less, never more), at most 10 active links per session, revocable at once, listed with created/expires/uses in the tracking view. The address is `/track#<token>`: the token sits in the URL **fragment**, so it reaches no access log, proxy or `Referer`, and the page strips it from the address bar. The page calls `POST /api/v1/telemetry/public/tracking/view` (token in the body, no login) and gets exactly: mower name and model, active or ended, start and end time, last position, stale since, the track since the alarm (at most 1500 points), the link's expiry and the server time. No account, no mower id, no serial, no controls, nothing about other mowers. Unknown, expired and revoked tokens are all `404`. Rate limit per client (rightmost `X-Forwarded-For`, else the peer): 60 requests per minute and 20 unusable tokens per minute, then `429` with `Retry-After`; the limiter is per replica and in memory (the token cannot be guessed, the limit protects the database). Every public answer carries `Cache-Control: no-store`, `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow, noarchive`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and a `default-src 'none'` CSP; the page itself is served by nginx with the same robots and referrer headers and `no-store`. A link outlives the end of the session until it expires (the police can look at the final track); it dies with the session's deletion.

**Theft report.** `GET /mowers/{id}/tracking/report.pdf?lang=de|en` builds a PDF on the server with **fpdf2** (pure Python, LGPL-3.0, which is compatible with the AGPL; it brings `defusedxml` and `fonttools` and uses Pillow, already in the lock file) and its built-in Helvetica: mower model, serial/device name, MowDeck name, alarm kind and time, last position, the track as vectors on a blank metre grid (no map tiles are fetched or embedded, nothing leaves the server), a position table (at most 45 rows, thinned), up to four camera photos, and blank owner fields (name, address, contact, police report number, insurance). Photos: Telemetry asks for one still image at the start and once more a minute later if the first failed (`gateway.mower.<id>.photo` with the error-analysis demand, which may wake a sleeping camera through the relay and ignores the Vision budget, [ADR 0045](0045-camera-relay.md); a lifted mower is an error state), plus `POST .../tracking/photo` by hand (at most 5 per session). It stores its own images in `tracking_photos`; the camera photo of push notifications ([ADR 0056](0056-camera-photo-in-push-notifications.md)) is a separate store and is not reused.

**Privacy and retention.** Ended sessions with their links and photos are deleted `..._RETENTION_DAYS` (30) after the end by the existing hourly retention job; `DELETE .../sessions/{id}` deletes an ended session at once. The positions stay in the mower's position track under its own retention (90 days, [ADR 0017](0017-telemetry-storage-and-retention.md)); a mower removal deletes everything of the mower.

**Base map (amended 2026-10-28).** The first version drew the track on the browser side tile server of the installation (`WEB_MAP_TILES`, off by default) and otherwise on a plain surface, which in dark mode is black: on a real installation the owner saw a black rectangle with a red line, useless for finding a mower that left the garden (and a stolen mower can be hundreds of kilometres away). The aerial photo proxy ([ADR 0054](0054-aerial-photo-under-the-map.md)) serves only the garden. So Telemetry now serves a base map **around the track**: layer `map`, a street map from an XYZ template (`MOWDECK_TELEMETRY_BASEMAP_URL`, `_ATTRIBUTION`, `_MIN_ZOOM` 8, `_MAX_ZOOM` 19, `_COVERAGE`), and layer `aerial`, the configured aerial photo source (`..._AERIAL_COVERAGE`). Both are off unless configured: no request to any third party unless the operator sets a source. The documented street map is **OpenStreetMap** (`https://tile.openstreetmap.org/{z}/{x}/{y}.png`, worldwide to zoom 19, owner decision); BKG TopPlusOpen (federal open data, street level only within Europe) stays documented as an alternative. A tile is served when it overlaps the box around the session's positions plus `..._TRACKING_MAP_MARGIN_METERS` (2000), lies in the layer's zoom range and in its coverage box; anything else is `404 tile-outside-map` before any upstream request, so it is no open proxy. Owner: `GET /mowers/{id}/tracking/map/{layer}/{z}/{x}/{y}` (`can_view`). Share link: `POST /public/tracking/tile` with token, layer and tile in the body (the token and the place never reach an access log; bad tokens count against the failure limit of the view, tiles have their own allowance of 600 per minute and client and do not count as views of the link). The tracking status and the public view list the offered layers (`map_layers`: id, credit line, zoom range, `covers_last` at the last position, the `coverage` box and the served `margin_m`), nothing else. The map keeps its view inside the box it gets tiles for: positions plus margin cut to the coverage as `maxBounds`, the lowest zoom at which that box still fills the view as `minZoom` (never below the layer's), the layer's `max_zoom` as `maxZoom`; the coverage box is installation configuration the credit line already names, so it reveals nothing about the owner. The view is fitted to the track only until the user moves the map in any way (a pinch fires no drag event, so the first version refitted on every poll after a pinch).

*OpenStreetMap tile usage policy.* Telemetry is the only client of the tile server and behaves as the policy asks: every upstream request carries `User-Agent: MowDeck/<version> (+https://github.com/lukislp/mowdeck)` and no `Referer`; a tile is kept as long as `Cache-Control: max-age` or `Expires` allow and never fetched again before that (without either for the configured cache TTL, at least 7 days; `no-store`/`no-cache` answers are not kept); only tiles a viewer is looking at are fetched (no prefetch, no bulk download, only around a track); new tiles are limited per account (240 per minute, four in flight) and on the share page per client; the credit line links to the copyright page. Fetching, the disk cache (its file time is the expiry) and the limits are shared with the aerial photo.

*Why server side, not the browser side tile setting:* the browser never contacts a third party, the content security policy stays as it is (blob URLs), the share page discloses nothing beyond its track, the cache serves every viewer, and the operator's choice applies to everyone.

*Wherever the mower is.* The map fits the whole track wherever it lies. A layer that does not cover the last position (`covers_last: false`), or whose whole round of tiles fails, is skipped for the next one with a hint ("Luftbild gibt es an dieser Position nicht"), and without any the track is drawn on a grid with a hint. Both pages show the last position as text (decimal degrees, six places, time and age of the fix) with a copy button and links to Apple Maps, Google Maps, OpenStreetMap and, on Android, `geo:`; the links are built from the coordinates only, so a share token never appears in them. The vendor reports no accuracy figure, so none is shown. The same change fixed the map component's redraw after the map is rebuilt (a statement had been swallowed by a comment).

**UI.** `/mowers/{id}/tracking` (phone first: large map with the layer switch "Karte" / "Luftbild", status, the position as text with copy and map links, "Link teilen", "Bericht (PDF)", "Ortung beenden" with the choice false alarm or stop, links list), the public page `/track`, a shield button and banner on the mower page, German and English texts. The tracking view polls the API every 8 s with `after=` (no new realtime message); the mower page every 30 s.

## Consequences

- The track depends on the gateway delivering reports: a mower out of coverage shows "stale since" and its last position, the track resumes when it reconnects. Without RTK the positions can be metres off; the report says so.
- The vendor cloud sees one report request per 10 s per tracked mower for at most 24 h (default). Lowering `interval_seconds` raises that; the adapter floor is 5 s.
- Share links are bearer secrets: whoever has the address sees the position until it expires or is revoked. The fragment keeps it out of logs, but not out of the chat where the owner pastes it.
- The rate limiter is per replica. Several Telemetry replicas multiply the allowance, which is still harmless for 256-bit tokens.
- The end-to-end test starts a session by hand (the same path an alarm takes); the lift itself cannot be triggered in the Compose stack, so alarm to session is covered by the Notifications and Telemetry integration tests on the bus.
- With a base map source the provider sees the server's address and the tiles around a tracked mower (not the viewers); the first view of a place costs some dozens of tile requests, later ones come from the cache.
- Not covered: ending a session from the notification (service worker action), mapping the vendor's real serial number, a realtime message instead of polling, photos on the share page.
