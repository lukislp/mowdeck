# ADR 0030: Mowing patterns as plug-ins and the Planner

## Status

Accepted (2026-10-10)

## Context

Phase 5 ("Spot & Kamera") brings spot mowing of any shape and re-mowing missed patches (project plan, "Mähmuster & Routen", "Spot-Mähen beliebiger Formen", "Verpasste Flecken nachmähen"). Patterns are plug-ins: input a polygon with holes, mower data and settings that describe themselves as JSON Schema; output a neutral mowing plan of segments (drive with the blades on or off at a speed, in order) that does not depend on the mower model. The same validation chain applies to every pattern (inside the area with a safety margin, the turning radius is feasible, no no-go zones, blades off on transfers), so a plug-in cannot produce an unsafe route. Patterns can be chained ("2 border laps + diagonal + re-mow") and saved as templates. The Planner computes, checks and previews (CPU-bound, "no own data", reusable as a library). Execution goes through the Device-Gateway: as the mower's own path sequence, or as a native job when the mower runs the pattern itself. The LUBA runs stripes at any angle, checkerboard, border laps and the order of both natively, but no arbitrary paths are known (spike 0001, section 3.2); `MowerCapabilities.custom_paths` is False for it, and `mowdeck_control.translation.plan_to_native_job` is the seam to native jobs ([ADR 0012](0012-mower-control-interface-and-safety-rules.md)).

## Decision

**`mowdeck-patterns` is a pure library (Apache-2.0) on Shapely 2.1.** No network, no database; it depends on `mowdeck-contracts` and `shapely>=2.1` only. All geometry is local metres of the mower's map (`MowerMap`, [ADR 0023](0023-map-rendering-and-projection.md)). The models: `Area` (boundary, holes, no-go zones, obstacles; at most 5000 vertices, 50 000 m² and 1 km across), `MowerSpec` (cutting width, overlap, turning radius, mowing and transfer speed, safety margin, time per turn), `MowPlan` (ordered `Segment{polyline, blades_on, speed, kind}` with `kind` `mow`, `turn` or `transfer`, `estimated_duration_s`, `covered_area_m2`, `area_m2`, `pattern_chain`, `warnings`). The spacing of runs is the cutting width minus the overlap; while cutting, the mower's centre keeps half the cutting width (at least the safety margin) from every edge, hole and zone, so the blade reaches the edge without the mower leaving the margin.

**A pattern is a class with `key`, `name`, `settings_schema()` and `compute(area, mower, settings)`** (the `MowPattern` protocol), registered as an entry point in the group `mowdeck.patterns` (name = key). The built-ins derive from `BasePattern`: settings are a Pydantic model (its JSON Schema is the form the app builds, the same model validates and refuses unknown keys), the pattern only orders runs, and the shared code joins runs with `turn`s (short, inside the cutting region, blades on) or routed `transfer`s (blades off, shortest way inside the safe region along a visibility graph of its corners), then estimates duration (driving time plus a fixed time per turn above 60 degrees) and coverage (blades-on segments widened to the cutting width, intersected with the mowable region). Built-in: `stripes`, `diagonal`, `checkerboard`, `spiral` (concentric around the outer shape, cut at holes), `border_fill` (laps, then any built-in fill), `contour` (laps parallel to every edge including holes), `auto_aligned` (the angle with the fewest runs among 5 degree steps and the longest edges), `cells` (slab decomposition through concave corners and hole corners, each cell at its own best angle), `alternating` (angle from a rotation list by `run_index`, or a random angle deterministic for `seed` and `run_index`), `edge_only` and `remow` (stripes over gap polygons, or over the area minus a covered polygon). Waves, circles, rays and lawn art are later. `chain()` runs patterns one after the other with blades-off transfers between them (at most eight).

**One validation chain for every plan.** `validate(plan, area, mower)` raises `PlanRejected(reasons)`: every segment inside the area minus the safety margin (holes and zones excluded); no blades-on segment through a no-go zone, an obstacle or a hole; transfers with the blades off; speeds within the mower's limits; and a turning heuristic: a heading change of `theta` needs `r * tan(theta / 2)` of straight track on both sides of the corner, a reversal is impossible for `r > 0` (mowers that turn on the spot use `r = 0`). `plan()` is compute plus validate and is what the Planner calls; a plug-in's output never reaches a mower unchecked. `coverage(track, width)` and `gaps(area, covered, min_area)` turn a position track into covered ground and the gaps left (slivers below 5 cm are opened away), `preview(plan)` gives a GeoJSON-like collection.

**Plans travel inline in the start command, not by reference.** The contract `SpotMowPlan{plan_hash, pattern_chain, segments, estimated_duration_s, covered_area_m2, native}` carries `SpotSegment{kind, blades_on, speed_mps, path}` where `path` is an encoded polyline (the map-services algorithm, centimetre precision): a plan of a few thousand points stays well below the bus message size. `plan_hash` is the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of chain and segments and must match, so a recorded hash identifies exactly one route. `StartMow` gains the optional `spot_plan` as a third target (exactly one of `area_ids`, `plan_ref`, `spot_plan`; old messages stay valid). The alternative, a plan store with `plan_ref`, would give the Planner data of its own and every consumer a dependency on it; inline plans need nothing but the contract.

**Execution decides by capabilities.** `MowerController` accepts a `spot_plan` when `capabilities.custom_paths` is true; otherwise only when the plan carries `native` (`SpotNativeJob{area_ids, pattern}`: the chain is one native pattern, or `border_fill` with a native fill and at most four laps, on a whole map area that the mower supports), and then sends the native `StartMow(area_ids, pattern)` instead, checked like any area start. Anything else is rejected ("custom paths not supported ..."). The simulator now reports `custom_paths=True` (option `custom_paths=False` simulates a LUBA-like mower) and follows the segments with their blades and speed; the Mammotion adapter keeps `custom_paths=False` and rejects a spot plan without a native job itself as well. The Device-Gateway passes the command through unchanged.

**Fleet records the hash, gates the feature, limits the size.** A `start_mow` with a `spot_plan` needs the plan feature `spot_mowing` (declared by the Planner, granted to `home` and `fleet`, `unlimited` has `*`) besides `control`, may be at most `MOWDECK_SPOT_PLAN_MAX_BYTES` (256 KiB) as JSON, is refused for bulk commands (a plan is computed for one mower's map), and its record keeps `plan_hash` (new column, `CommandView.plan_hash`) and a summary instead of the segments, so the command audit stays small.

**The Planner service** (`services/planner`, AGPL) is a thin HTTP layer: `GET /patterns` (registry with schemas and the native hint), `POST /plans/preview` (mower or raw map, a map area and/or a spot polygon in local metres or WGS84, a chain or a saved template, optional mower data overrides; answers totals, warnings, the preview and how the mower can run it: `custom_paths`, `native`, `unsupported` or `unknown`), `POST /plans` (the same plus the `SpotMowPlan` and the ready `StartMow` for Fleet; nothing is stored, the same request gives the same hash), `POST /coverage/gaps` and templates. It keeps a directory of map and capability snapshots from the `DEVICE` stream (durable consumer, newest message per subject first, like the Scheduler and Telemetry), converts WGS84 with the map's geo reference, and refuses drawn spots outside the mapped areas (the mower knows nothing beyond them). Geometry runs in worker threads, at most `MOWDECK_PLANNER_MAX_PARALLEL` per replica. Previews need `can_view` on the mower, plans `can_control` (Fleet checks again), every planning endpoint the plan feature `spot_mowing`; `planner.view` is an account key implied by `mower.view`.

**Coverage over the bus.** Services do not call each other over HTTP, and subscribing to every position would be heavy, so Telemetry answers a core NATS request `PositionTrackRequest{account_id, mower_id, start, end, limit}` with `PositionTrackReply{count, track, first_at, last_at, truncated, error}` (the track an encoded polyline of latitude and longitude, seven decimals, up to 50 000 points). The subject is `tracks.positions.query`, deliberately not `telemetry.positions.query`: the `TELEMETRY` stream captures `telemetry.>`, and a request on a stream subject is answered by JetStream's publish acknowledgement instead of the responder. The track has no blade state; everything driven in the chosen time range counts as covered, jumps over 3 m break the track.

**Deviation from "no own data": templates.** Templates are per account and must survive restarts, so the Planner owns a small PostgreSQL database (`templates`, plus the snapshot directory `mower_facts`) instead of storing JSON in Fleet, which would couple a planning concept to the mower registry. Plans themselves are still never stored. Templates hold only pattern keys and settings; every principal of the account reads them, saving and deleting needs `can_control` on the account.

## Consequences

- A new pattern is a class plus an entry point; the Planner, the app (form from the schema) and the validation chain need no change. Gating individual patterns by plan stays possible (one feature key per pattern) but is not done yet; `spot_mowing` gates them all.
- Mowers without `custom_paths` (the LUBA today) run only whole-area native plans; spot polygons, spirals, contours, cells and re-mowing need a mower or adapter that follows paths. The LUBA's path following remains an open question of the spike.
- Coverage counts transfers as mowed (no blade state in the position track); choose the job's time range. A blade flag in the track would make it exact.
- Plans are computed for the map at request time; a map changed in the vendor app between preview and start is not detected by the hash. The simulator and the controller check segments only for blades-off transfers, the Planner's validation chain is the safety check.
- Coordinates of spot plans are rounded to centimetres. Patterns keep an extra centimetre from the margins, and the Planner runs the validation chain on the plan before and after rounding.

## Amendment (2026-10-14): mowing defaults per mower in Fleet

**Decision.** The settings of a native start that every client offers are one contract, `MowPattern{name, angle_deg, border_laps, spacing_m}` (the common, validated part of `StartMow.pattern`; adapters may accept further keys), and Fleet keeps them per mower as the mowing defaults: `PUT /api/v1/fleet/mowers/{id}/mow-defaults` with `{"pattern": MowPattern | null}` (`can_control`, 404 semantics; unknown keys and values out of range are 422; `null` drops the defaults), `MowerOut.mow_defaults` (`{"pattern": {...}}` or `null`) on every mower answer, the audit action `mower.mow_defaults_changed` only when the value changes, and `TargetMower.mow_pattern` in the reply of `fleet.targets.resolve`, so the Scheduler starts from the same defaults as the web start sheet and Home Assistant. Without saved defaults every client uses stripes at 0 degrees with one border lap. Nothing is sent to the mower: a start still names its own pattern, and whether the mower runs it is decided with `MowerCapabilities.native_patterns`. Inside Fleet the defaults are the domain value `MowDefaults{pattern: MowPattern}`; the stored shape (`mowers.mow_defaults`, nullable JSONB `{"pattern": {...}}` without unset optional keys, migration 0009) belongs to the database adapter alone, and a row this version cannot read counts as "no defaults".

Alternatives: keeping the settings in the browser would lose them across devices and would leave Home Assistant and the Scheduler without them. Planner templates are account-wide pattern chains for spot plans, not the native start settings of one mower, and the Scheduler would then depend on the Planner to issue a start. A setpoint sent to the mower does not exist: the LUBA takes the pattern per job, not as a persistent setting. One pattern per mower rather than per area keeps the start sheet and the schedule form small; per-area defaults can be added as a further key later.

Consequences: a pattern the mower does not support can be saved (the defaults describe what the user wants; the start sheet offers only what `native_patterns` reports and the start is checked by the controller). `spacing_m` stays `null` unless the user sets it, so the model's own spacing remains the default. The schedule form still edits only the angle and the rotation list; the other settings come from the defaults.
