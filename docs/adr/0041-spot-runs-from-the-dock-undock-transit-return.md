# ADR 0041: Spot runs from the dock: undock, transit and return

## Status

Accepted (2026-10-15)

## Context

A spot run (ADR 0039) started only from an idle mower on the lawn, in the same area as the spot and at most 10 m away from it. The owner had to drive the mower out of its charging station with the joystick first and bring it near the spot, and afterwards drive it home or send it home in the vendor app. The owner wants a run to start by itself from the charging station, drive to the spot, mow it and go back to the dock, also when the spot lies in another garden behind several transfer paths.

The map already holds everything needed, in the neutral form of `MowerMap`: the areas, the transfer paths between them (driven with the blades off), the no-go zones, the obstacles and the dock pose. The mower can leave its charging station by itself (on Mammotion: PyMammotion's `leave_dock`, the one-touch undocking of the app) and return to it by itself (the generic `ReturnToDock`).

## Decision

A spot run gets three new phases around the spot itself: it undocks, drives a *transit* with the blades off and sends the mower home. Everything except the undocking call is generic.

**Capability `undock`.** `MowerCapabilities.undock` says that a connection can make the mower leave its charging station by itself; the connection then implements the optional protocol `UndockCapable` with `leave_dock()`. It only sends: the mower drives a short way out of the dock and reports `idle`, the run watches the state. The simulator implements it (from the dock it drives `undock_distance_m`, 1 m, straight out along `dock.heading_deg` and stands idle; option `undock`, default on). The Mammotion adapter sends PyMammotion's `leave_dock` (`MctlNav(todev_one_touch_leave_pile=1)`) once with `Priority.USER`, only from the dock, and boosts its refresh loop like after the other job commands. On a dual link it goes over the cloud: only the drive channel uses the bridge (ADR 0038, link roles).

**The transit planner** (`mowdeck_control/transit.py`, pure Python, no I/O, no geometry library; `mowdeck-control` keeps its standard-library-only rule). `plan_transit(map, start, goal)` finds the route from the mower to the first point of the plan:

- *Drivable space* (`DrivableSpace`): the areas inset by 0.3 m, the transfer paths widened to corridors of ±0.5 m, a disc of 1.5 m around the dock (where an undocked mower stands), minus every no-go zone and obstacle buffered by 0.3 m.
- *Graph*: the start and the goal; the path vertices (simplified by 5 cm, then at most 1 m apart); the concave corners of every area and the convex corners of every zone, pushed 0.2 m beyond the margin into the free side; and *portals*, the nearest point inside an area's inset for a node just outside it (a path end on the lawn's edge, the mower in front of the dock). Edges: consecutive path vertices (the corridor's centre line, clear of zones); straight connections inside one area between its corners, portals, path entries, the start and the goal (an exact test: the segment keeps 0.3 m from the area's edge and from every zone); and connectors of at most 2 m between any two nodes that stay in the drivable space (sampled every 5 cm): a path end to its portal, two paths that meet, the start to a nearby path.
- *Search*: A* (Dijkstra with the straight-line distance as the lower bound) with lazily checked edges. A route may chain several transfer paths and pass through intermediate areas. No route, a start or goal outside the drivable space, or a route longer than `max_length_m` (default 300 m) raises `NoRouteError`; the run is then refused before anything moves, with the reason `no_route: ...`.

**The run's phases** (`SpotRunStatus.state`): `preparing`, `undocking`, `waiting_rtk`, `transit`, `spot_mowing` (with `paused_rtk` as before), `returning`, then `done`, `aborted` or `failed`.

- *Undocking.* A docked or charging mower on a connection with `undock` gets `leave_dock` (no drive frames, so no drive session). The run waits until the mower reports `idle` for 3 s with two consecutive reports closer than 15 cm (`undocking`), then for a fixed RTK position no older than 10 s (`waiting_rtk`). After 90 s (`undock_timeout_s`) it aborts with `undock_failed`, or `rtk_lost` when it was already out and waiting for the fix. While undocking only an error, an update or a lost mower end the run; a mower leaving its dock may report anything else. Without `undock` a docked mower is refused as before.
- *Transit.* Before the start the transit is planned from the dock (a docked mower) or from the reported position (any other mower, which still needs a fixed RTK position at the start). After undocking it is planned again from the real pose. The route is prepended as a blades-off leg driven by the existing pure-pursuit loop at `transit_speed_mps` (0.3 m/s at most). Its geofence is the drivable space, checked on every report and every dead-reckoned estimate; the spot's own geofence (areas inset 0.3 m minus the zones) applies again from the plan's first point. The drive session (Bluetooth on demand through the bridge) opens with the first transit frame, after undocking. The 10 m approach limit is gone: the transit replaces the old straight approach leg (a straight line is simply the shortest route when it is clear).
- *Spot mowing.* As in ADR 0039. The run-time limit counts from the start of the transit: `min(2 × estimated + 60 s, 20 min)` for the plan plus twice the transit's driving time, so a long transit gets its own share.
- *Returning.* With `SpotMow.return_to_dock` (default true) the run sends the generic `ReturnToDock` to the connection after `done` and after every abort that left the mower healthy (`geofence`, `rtk_lost`, `timeout`, `heading_mismatch`, `frame_rejected`, `link_lost` from a refused frame, `no_route`, `undock_failed`), unless the mower already stands in its dock. Never after a stop by the user (`spot_stop`: the mower just stops), an emergency stop, a sensor stop, a change of activity or an internal error. The mower navigates home by itself; the run is `returning` while the command is sent (at most 30 s) and then ends. The report says `sent_home` or why the return was not sent.

Safety stays first: the blades are off during undocking, transit and return in every case (only `mow` and `turn` segments of the plan may switch them on, as before); every end of the drive part sends a zero frame, switches the blades off and closes the drive session; rule 12 keeps every other command out until the run ended.

**Start conditions** (controller rule 12 and `spot_start_problem`). A `spot_mow` may now be sent to a docked or charging mower when the connection has `undock` (and implements `UndockCapable`); RTK is then not required at the start because the run waits for it after undocking. Unchanged: more than 20 % battery, a fresh state, no job, no drive session or pending confirmation, the plan inside the geofence, and while a run is active only `emergency_stop`, `spot_stop` and `set_light`. The reachability check (`no_route`) runs in the Device-Gateway's controller before the command is accepted, so Fleet's record and the web app get a clear `rejected` with the reason.

**The web app and the gateway agree on the route.** The sheet's condition "Route to the spot found" replaces "same area, within 10 m". It is computed with the same algorithm as the gateway: the Planner's `POST /plans/preview` takes an optional `transit` (`{from_dock: true}` for a docked mower that can undock, else `{position: [x, y]}` in local metres, rounded to 0.5 m) and returns `transit{found, from_dock, length_m, route, reason}` from `mowdeck_control.transit.plan_transit` on the mower's map snapshot, the same map the gateway's controller holds. This respects ADR 0030: the browser asks the Planner over its public API as before, no service calls another over HTTP, and the gateway plans on its own. The preview draws the found route as a `transfer` before the start; during the run the gateway's path snapshot carries the transit as the first `transfer` polyline. A docked mower that can undock counts as ready and its RTK row as green (the run waits for the fix after undocking). The run bar shows the new states, and the reasons `undock_failed` and `no_route` in German and English.

**Owner script.** `try_real_mower.py --spot-dry-run --from-dock` runs the whole cycle on the real mower with the blades off: it prints the planned transit, sends `leave_dock`, drives the transit and a 1 m circle around `--spot-at X,Y` (or the centre of `--spot-area`, or of the area nearest to the dock) and sends the mower home.

## Manufacturer independence

Generic, the same for every manufacturer: the capability flag `undock` and the protocol `UndockCapable`, the transit planning on the neutral `MowerMap` (`plan_transit`, `DrivableSpace`), the run phases (undocking wait, RTK wait, transit with its geofence and time share, return), the start conditions in the controller, the Planner's transit preview, the web conditions and labels, and the simulator's implementation. The spot-run and transit tests in `mowdeck-control`, the gateway's and the Planner's tests import no manufacturer package.

Adapter work, once per manufacturer: the `leave_dock()` call that makes the mower leave its charging station, and `capabilities.undock`. The return uses the generic `ReturnToDock` every adapter already has. For Mammotion that is one PyMammotion command sent over the cloud.

## Consequences

Easier: a spot anywhere in the mapped gardens can be mown with one tap from the charging station, also behind several transfer paths, and the mower comes home by itself. The web app shows the route before the start and refuses a start the gateway would refuse. Harder: the run lasts longer and depends on the map's paths being drivable; a transit is driven at 1 Hz reports like the spot itself, so it is slow (300 m take about 17 minutes at 0.3 m/s). The transit's corridor of ±0.5 m around a path leaves little room for steering errors; a geofence abort then sends the mower home with its own navigation.

To check on hardware before the first real run: what the LUBA reports while it leaves the dock (activity, how far it drives out, how long until `idle`), whether the undocked pose lies within 1.5 m of the mapped dock pose (the dock apron of the drivable space), the report rate of an idle mower right after undocking without a drive session (the boost after `leave_dock` should give a report every few seconds; the transit pauses until reports are fresh), whether the dock's `heading_deg` points out of the dock, and the pure-pursuit tracking along a real transfer path corridor. A `spot_stop` while the mower undocks ends the run but does not interrupt the mower's own undocking manoeuvre (about a metre, no blades); whether the emergency stop halts it on the LUBA is part of the same check.
